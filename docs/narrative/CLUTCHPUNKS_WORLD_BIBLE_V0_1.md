## 1. Identity and story promise

**Game / umbrella universe:** CLUTCHPUNKS (creator-approved name, 1 November 2026).
**Season 01 title:** THE FEED IS BROKEN (working season canon; narrative detail pending review).
**Genre and voice:** colorful high-impact cartoon arcade action, satirical internet culture, character-driven rebellion and an escalating mystery.
**Core promise:** Three unlikely hero archetypes stand up to a system that turns attention into reality. They can win a battle against a monster made of hate; winning the right to be seen is harder.
**Working campaign line:** "Built Different. Play Different." (not yet creator-approved tagline).
**Working story thesis:** "In a world obsessed with attention, being yourself is an act of rebellion."
**Rule of tone:** Comedy on the surface; character stakes underneath. Never write characters as interchangeable meme references or a brand that lectures its audience.

### Non-negotiable production boundary

This is narrative/worldbuilding material. It **does not** approve additional maps, bosses, NPC dialogue systems, cinematics, seasonal retention, accounts, rewards, combat mechanics, enemies, monetization, backend, multiplayer, or public launch. M0 is the restricted Web solo test in Ruin Alley. M1 is the public Web + Discord alpha with its separately approved operational backend and feedback capability. Every later playable level needs a new GDD gameplay contract and a separate production decision.

## 2. The one-sentence world pitch

**"The internet went viral; then the world did."**

In the city, every signal carries stories, opinions and reactions. After an unexplained event called **The Incident**, amplified attention starts imprinting digital behavior onto the physical world: commentary becomes danger, trends mutate into threats, and parts of the city begin to lose their history. A ubiquitous distribution system called **The Feed** may be responding to a fault — or may be behaving exactly as its creators intended.

### The central mystery

- **Surface question, Season 01:** Why did the world begin to break, and how can the local broadcast be stopped?
- **Midpoint question:** Who benefits when attention produces fear, conflict and monsters?
- **Season-finale question:** Who built the distribution network, and why does its control signal point toward Crown Court?
- **Series question:** Can the heroes free the world from a system that defines what is worth remembering?

The answer to the ultimate series question must remain genuinely open. Do not imply the website or Season 01 already proves who invented The Feed.

## 3. Suggested physics of the fiction (not gameplay rules)

These are **story metaphors and proposed lore**, not simulation formulas.

1. **Attention amplifies:** Mass attention strengthens a story's manifestation in places touched by the broken signal. Attention is not identical to XP, Aura, power level, damage, loot, currencies or account progression.
2. **The signal can distort:** Glitches, visual cuts, looped chatter and physical manifestations indicate a region under Feed interference. Normal people need not understand how this happens.
3. **Memory can fray:** The most disturbing interference is an echo: reports of a person, event or district vanish or become mutually contradictory. This is a story stake, **not** a character-permadeath or save-deletion mechanic.
4. **Heroes keep their shape:** The heroes resist being defined entirely by public reactions. They are not immune to damage or boss abilities; their resilience is a narrative characterization.
5. **The Feed is not Big Hater:** Big Hater exploits the system in Ruin Alley. He is an antagonist and beneficiary, not established as the system's original creator.
6. **Signals have geography:** Ruin Alley is where the story begins. Proposed later districts can expose different functions of the network. Crown Court and Toxic Garden are future-world references only, already identified as **non-playable M0/M1 references** in the GDD.
7. **A win is real, but local:** Beating a boss matters. It does not resolve the whole world or erase other regions' histories.

### Why "Aura" still matters

Aura is a **hero resource** in the approved game: its single meter charges a manually activated hero-specific Flex. It must remain explicitly distinct from **XP Orbs and run levels**, the wave clock, and any imaginary notion of social-media points. In dialogue, "aura" may colloquially refer to unshakable presence, but never suggest followers/likes fuel the actual gameplay meter.

### Why repeat runs?

**Public narrative framing:** "Every run is another live attempt to hold the signal." This is a fiction wrapper for roguelite retry, not canonical time travel, shared-world persistence, or an authority to change save logic. Only a completed real game run produces real result data.

## 4. Character identity contract

The game provides **three canonical gameplay kits with six selectable presentations**. The pairings and gameplay identities are fixed by the GDD:

| Canonical kit ID | Selectable appearances | Class and starter | Flex | Narrative role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sigma` | The Sigma / Pixel Vixen | Ranged; No Cap | Deadpan Verdict | The operator who sees the pattern under the noise |
| `cool-kid` | Cool Kid / Byte Baddie | Melee; Keyboard Warrior | CTRL + CHAOS | The improviser who refuses to obey the script |
| `alpha` | Alpha Chad / Block Queen | Tank; Cope Shield | Giga Chad Rampage | The defender who stands between others and the swarm |

**Hard rule:** The female presentations are not additional hero kits, unlocks or lore-mandated independent teammates. Do not write a canonical six-person ensemble, sibling/backstory relationship, parallel universe or twin encounter into the actual game without a separate creator decision. A shared story beat must work with either appearance of a chosen kit. Voice/flavor variants may be pitched but must not promise separate mechanics or change canon silently.

### Character arcs proposed for Season 01

- **The operator (Sigma / Pixel Vixen):** Begins convinced that detachment and precision are enough. Discovers that recognizing a system's manipulation creates a responsibility to act. **Temptation:** be so cool that they never intervene until too late.
- **The improviser (Cool Kid / Byte Baddie):** Begins enjoying the absurd attention. Realizes that making a spectacle is precisely what feeds the machine. **Temptation:** become the viral sensation instead of breaking the cycle.
- **The defender (Alpha Chad / Block Queen):** Begins believing strength solves everything. Learns that protection also means preserving someone else's voice, not simply absorbing a hit. **Temptation:** solve a structural problem with more force.

### Dialogue discipline

- Operator: short, precise, quietly cutting. Example: "Three patterns. One source."
- Improviser: quick, energetic, jokes under pressure. Example: "Chat, that was NOT in the patch notes."
- Defender: minimal, grounded, reassuring. Example: "Stay behind me. You still get a say."
- Avoid constant slang-dumping, real streamer impersonations, copyrighted quotes or unlicensed meme audio.
- For website static copy, narrative events may be described from a neutral narrator so all three roles remain possible protagonists.

## 5. Antagonists and forces

### Big Hater — Season 01 initial threat, canon boss

A loud, obsessive force of resentment that thrives when everyone is fighting for attention. **Proposed narrative motive:** He keeps Ruin Alley caught in a loop of outrage because he cannot tolerate someone else standing out. He believes defeating the heroes will prove that the Feed's rules favor him.

**Do not alter** the approved final boss's 10:00 appearance, attack sequence, HP, Berserk timing or failure/retry rules. His story escalation can be expressed through artwork, voice, banners and approved cosmetic feedback only.

### The ordinary swarm — canonical enemies, narrative interpretations

- **NPCs:** people/avatars following the loudest signal.
- **Haters:** weaponized heckling that attacks from a distance.
- **Reply Goblins:** fast multiplying argument chains.
- **Beta Bots and Ratio Rams:** brute enforcement of attention dynamics.
- **Doomscrollers, Hype Bots and support enemies:** feedback loops and amplification.
- **Comment Mods:** attempts to suppress opposing voices.
- The GDD's actual enemy names, patterns, timings and visual masters take precedence over this interpretation.

### Event bosses — existing gameplay identities

**Reply Guy**, **Mod Supremo**, **Ohio Express** and **Doomscroll Prime** are the four canonical Ruin Alley events. They provide the Act I escalation; none is retconned into an alternate final boss. The story should give them personality without reassigning scheduled encounters.

### Suggested future opposition (not approved gameplay)

- **The Echo Collective:** remnants that repeat the last message they heard, relevant to the proposed Echo District.
- **The Null Wardens:** entities that remove or overwrite evidence of past events, relevant to the proposed Shadow Zone.
- **The Patch Office:** a bureaucratic layer making the Feed's edits look like harmless maintenance, relevant to Signal Core.
- **The Broadcast Custodian:** a Season 01 finale adversary who defends the local transmission and implies a higher hierarchy; **name, visual appearance, mechanics and even boss status are deliberately undecided**.
- **The Crown Protocol:** a hinted governing layer associated with Crown Court; this is a proposed Season 02 premise, not proof of who controls the system.

## 7. Voice, humor and responsible parody

**Creative pillars**
1. **Character before reference.** A joke should still land even when a specific meme ages.
2. **Tonal contrast.** A ridiculous enemy can create a real threat. The punchline must not erase consequences.
3. **Satirize systems, not vulnerable people.** Mock attention economies, performative arguments and absurd power structures; avoid punching down at player identities.
4. **Short in combat.** One-line boss calls, phase banners and visual storytelling; no long unskippable cutscenes.
5. **Readable abroad.** English-first production copy with localization-ready strings; do not make the premise reliant on understanding one country's platform-specific slang.
6. **Original expression.** No licensed social platform logos, real-person likenesses, copyrighted meme characters or copied dialogue without a separate licensing review.

**Presentation references** may include kinetic cartoons, comic panels and street-broadcast aesthetics, but do not recreate protected assets or a competitor's characters.

## 8. Canon, reveal and spoiler governance

| Truth level | What visitors may learn | How it is labeled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public / Chapter 00 | Ruin Alley is behaving impossibly; the Feed is implicated; heroes are going live | Teaser and prologue |
| Chapter 01 aftermath | Big Hater is defeatable; the corruption continues beyond him | Epilogue after story release, not the initial landing's hero section |
| Chapters 02–03 | The threat is distributed; memory/evidence distortion is intentional-looking | Subsequent releases |
| Chapters 04–05 | The local signal has an operator and points toward Crown Court | Finale reveal |
| Series mystery | Origin and ultimate intent of the Feed | Deliberately unresolved |

**Spoiler policy:** The website may be publicly accessible before the game; avoid exposing Big Hater's fate, future map specifics or the Crown Court revelation in the primary landing. Chapter-specific archive entries can visibly declare spoiler scope. No gating dependent on unverified player account data.

3. Are the proposed future regional names (Echo District, Shadow Zone, Signal Core, The Last Broadcast) accepted as narrative placeholders?
4. Should the finale adversary have a distinct named identity, or remain an unseen antagonist until Season 02?

6. Which Season 01 plot reveals should remain secret from pre-release marketing?
