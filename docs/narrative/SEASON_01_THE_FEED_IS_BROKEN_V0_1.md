# CLUTCHPUNKS — Season 01: THE FEED IS BROKEN

**Narrative Bible v0.1 — story draft for creator review**
**Date:** 1 November 2026
**Approval status:** Game name **CLUTCHPUNKS** is creator-approved. The season title and overall direction are creator-supported. This document's chapter scripts, future locations, characters, adversaries, interludes and revelations are **unapproved narrative proposals**.
**Gameplay authority:** [GDD V3.0](../GDD.md); [World Bible](CLUTCHPUNKS_WORLD_BIBLE_V0_1.md).
**Compatibility:** Chapter 01 maps to the **existing** Ruin Alley 5 × 120 s solo waves and Big Hater at 10:00. Chapters 02–05 do not add current playable content.

## 1. Season logline and dramatic engine

**Logline:** When an algorithmic broadcast starts turning online hostility into physical chaos, a defiant local hero goes live in Ruin Alley, defeats a monster empowered by outrage and discovers that the city's collapse is not a glitch but a controlled transmission.

**Season question:** Can a person hold onto their identity when the world rewards whoever shouts the loudest?
**Emotional movement:** Ridicule → defiance → confidence → disillusionment → solidarity → deliberate rebellion.
**Series revelation:** The local signal is an output of a much larger infrastructure; the trace ends at **Crown Court**, not at Big Hater.

**Core dramatic irony:** Hater thinks he runs the Feed because it rewards his rage. The heroes initially think defeating him will fix it. Both are wrong.

### Canon and delivery split

- **Approved play today:** solo Ruin Alley; Sigma / Pixel Vixen, Cool Kid / Byte Baddie, Alpha Chad / Block Queen presentations; canonical weapons, events and final Big Hater.
- **Proposed story-only deliverable:** public **Chapter 00: The Incident** marketing website, separately reviewed for spoiler, accessibility, licensed/approved art and publication.
- **Proposed future playable maps:** Echo District, Shadow Zone, Signal Core and Last Broadcast are **narrative placeholders**, each requiring an independent GDD-approved gameplay/level/boss/asset contract before any implementation.
- **Season-structure promise, not release schedule:** chapters are staged creative units, **not** a commitment to launch dates or seasonal monetization.

## 2. Characters and relationships

The story can be experienced with **any one of the three existing canonical hero kits**, each presented as either its original or female appearance. Do not depict all six as independently playable cast members or write two simultaneous versions of a kit into canon. Website hero profiles may showcase all six as appearance choices, not as six confirmed allies.

### A. The operator — The Sigma / Pixel Vixen

**Want:** Prove that clear thinking cuts through the noise.
**Need:** Learn that noticing manipulation is not enough; silence also has consequences.
**Crisis:** A flawless tactical choice would protect them while abandoning the next neighborhood to the signal.
**Resolution:** Shares evidence openly, even when doing so draws the Feed's attention.
**Sample English copy:** "Patterns don't lie. Feeds do."

### B. The improviser — Cool Kid / Byte Baddie

**Want:** Turn a chaotic feed into the biggest live moment ever.
**Need:** Stop measuring value by how many people are watching.
**Crisis:** A stunt would guarantee attention but deepen the interference.
**Resolution:** Chooses a useful, unglamorous act over a viral highlight.
**Sample English copy:** "Okay, chat. New plan: no more playing their game."

### C. The defender — Alpha Chad / Block Queen

**Want:** Keep everyone safe by taking the hardest hits.
**Need:** Recognize that people cannot be protected by making all their decisions for them.
**Crisis:** Can hold a defensive position or help the public reclaim their own voice.
**Resolution:** Makes room for ordinary people to speak, rather than acting as their permanent shield.
**Sample English copy:** "Your voice. Your call. I've got your back."

### Supporting roles (fiction-only, no new runtime NPC requirement)

- **The Anonymous Broadcaster:** a fragmented signal that appears to warn viewers. Intent remains unclear. May be one person, an automated loop or a trap.
- **The Witnesses:** unnamed residents whose small contradictory accounts make the threat personal. Website text profiles, not new required NPC gameplay models.
- **The System Voice:** sterile intermittent announcements that euphemize suppression as "corrections" or "quality updates." Distinct from the meme-driven enemy voices.

### Character consistency

Each chapter should feature (a) one active objective, (b) one personality decision, (c) one consequence, (d) one piece of evidence. An individual player's chosen presentation must not determine access to lore or reveal a different required boss.

## 3. Detailed chapter outline

### CHAPTER 00 — THE INCIDENT (website prologue; no gameplay dependency)

**Dramatic purpose:** Invite visitors into a city that is beginning to malfunction. Make the player curious enough to meet a hero and enter Ruin Alley, without requiring a video or backend.

**Opening image:** A deceptively ordinary city broadcast. Local posts repeat, timestamps disagree, and a street camera briefly shows comments casting shadows on the pavement. The page initially feels like a hacked neighborhood bulletin, not a game shop.

**Scene 00.1 — Ordinary noise**
A local feed reports routine street life. One innocuous clip appears with the caption: **"This wasn't in the upload."** Replies multiply faster than the original post. An address and timestamp no longer agree.

**Scene 00.2 — First impossible report**
A witness describes a heckle striking a wall. Another claims a person vanished from a photograph. The page marks reports **UNVERIFIED** rather than asserting a real-world emergency.

**Scene 00.3 — The corrupted feed**
The site introduces a simple branded visual disruption: a repeated sentence, abrupt typography cut and a short red interruption. Motion stays optional. The line emerges:
**"EVERYTHING GETS SEEN. NOT EVERYTHING GETS TO STAY."**

**Scene 00.4 — Meet the response**
Introduce three hero **kits**, with two appearance options each. Their biographies explain attitude and starter weapon/Flex using GDD names; no fabricated distinct kits or stats for female appearances. For each card, one personal response to the anomaly.

**Scene 00.5 — An intercepted transmission**
An unknown broadcaster warns: **"Ruin Alley. 00:00. Don't believe what the Feed calls a fix."**
The site invites visitors to read the teaser / learn about the upcoming restricted test. A **PLAY** action must only exist when the user actually has authorized access to a verified build.

**Chapter 00 closing hook:** An audience counter freezes; a final incoming message reads: **"SOMEONE JUST WENT LIVE."**

**Non-spoiler promise:** The homepage does not reveal Big Hater's defeat, future levels, Crown Court's role or the underlying source of The Feed.

### CHAPTER 01 — GOING LIVE (existing Ruin Alley game run)

**Dramatic purpose:** Convert the player from bystander to participant; provide a full short-run arc and a decisive local victory or honest defeat.

**No new schedule:** Existing five two-minute waves and four event bosses stay intact. The story labels below **interpret** current encounters, not change their timing.

| Canonical phase | Clock | Narrative beat | Proposed presentation, only after separate approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First Post | 00:00–02:00 | The first broadcast draws a mob; a Hater begins attacking at 01:00 | Short "GOING LIVE" banner; a one-line ambient comment |
| Comment Section | 02:00–04:00 | The crowd becomes an argument; **Reply Guy at 02:15** | "ACTUALLY..." entry line, minimal text |
| Going Viral | 04:00–06:00 | A moderator intervenes; **Mod Supremo at 04:15** | "THIS POST HAS BEEN REMOVED" on a small broadcast strip |
| Cancel Storm | 06:00–08:00 | The attack goes public; **Ohio Express at 06:15**; legendary offer at 06:00 | Brief threat banner, never conceal telegraphs/cards |
| Final Ratio | 08:00–10:00 | The system deploys its most consuming pattern; **Doomscroll Prime at 08:15** | "YOU ARE STILL BEING WATCHED" line |
| Big Hater | 10:00 onward | A personal confrontation with the face of Ruin Alley's outrage | Existing 09:30 warning and boss mechanics retained |

**Boss voice pitch** (all localized-capable and optional; avoid dialogue over important telegraphs):
- Reply Guy: **"ACTUALLY—"**
- Mod Supremo: **"COMMENT REMOVED."**
- Ohio Express: **"NO BRAKES. NO CONTEXT."**
- Doomscroll Prime: **"KEEP SCROLLING."**
- Big Hater on entry: **"YOU DON'T GET TO BE THE MAIN CHARACTER."**
- Big Hater at Berserk: **"FULL HATER MODE."** (already the canonical phase name)

**Victory resolution:** Big Hater is defeated. The feed stops screaming for one quiet breath. The street's signs become readable. The hero can finally hear ordinary human voices. Then an old monitor continues broadcasting a message it should no longer be able to send.

**Proposed post-victory sting:**
> "LOCAL HOST DISCONNECTED."
>
> "UPSTREAM SIGNAL: ACTIVE."
>
> "THIS WAS NEVER A GLITCH."

**Defeat resolution:** The city does not end; another attempt may begin. No fake win, saved result, account reward, lore unlock or forced canonical victory. The website can explain "Ruin Alley is still transmitting" without exposing a player's personal run.

**Important:** Any in-game text, UI, new scene, sounds or cutscene has **not** been authorized by this document; it is a candidate presentation overlay to scope and review later, subject to M0 polish/performance priorities.

### CHAPTER 02 — THE SPREAD (proposed Echo District)

**Dramatic purpose:** Prove that Hater was a symptom, not the origin; widen the stakes from a street to a district.

**New environment concept:** **Echo District** — a neighborhood where screens repeat the same few seconds and every whispered opinion gets copied until the original is drowned out. Neon billboards contradict handwritten signs.

**Opening beat:** The heroes trace Ruin Alley's last transmission to a district transmitting the same footage with different people in it.

**Conflict:** Witnesses cannot agree who sent the first emergency message. A repeating crowd mistakes every unique statement for a threat to the pattern.

**Escalation:** A seemingly helpful automated relay offers to stabilize the district if the heroes accept that only "approved" memories should remain.

**Choice / growth:** The hero refuses a quick fix that would erase inconvenient testimony. This decision establishes the season's moral stakes.

**Proposed future boss concept:** **Echo Maw**, a creature shaped from repeated crowd reactions. **Visual, attacks, numeric rules, rewards, name and existence are all unapproved**; do not assume production.

**Ending clue:** Every repeating message contains a hidden checksum. The signature matches the same system process as Ruin Alley, but with a new designation: **NULL / REDACTION**.

**Playable status:** Story-only concept. No level design, wave duration, spawn rate or boss implementation contract exists.

### CHAPTER 03 — THE SHADOW (proposed Shadow Zone)

**Dramatic purpose:** Shift from public chaos to quiet unease. Introduce the danger of being erased rather than merely attacked.

**New environment concept:** **Shadow Zone** — a district of silent murals, blank digital advertisements and maps that disagree about what buildings exist.

**Opening beat:** A witness sends proof of a vanished street, but the transmission deletes the sender's name.

**Conflict:** The heroes pursue a trail of edits, discovering that the system is rewriting the record after each public incident.

**Midpoint revelation:** The Feed's "corrections" are neither random nor limited to viral monsters. Somebody has designed a process for removing inconvenient evidence from public memory.

**Character turn:** The cool observer must share their files; the show-off must stop performing; the protector must trust the people at risk to tell their own story.

**Proposed future boss concept:** **Null Warden**, an enforcement figure that suppresses traces rather than acting like a troll. Full encounter design unapproved.

**Ending clue:** The redaction pipeline has a maintenance address. It points beneath the city's central broadcast infrastructure.

**Playable status:** Story-only concept, no new gameplay authority.

### CHAPTER 04 — THE REVELATION (proposed Signal Core)

**Dramatic purpose:** Reveal the mechanism behind the local corruption without explaining the entire universe.

**New environment concept:** **Signal Core** — the city's hidden distribution plant, a dramatic place of cable forests, signal tunnels, editing chambers and impossible screens. It should feel like the machinery behind a lie, not generic sci-fi laboratory art.

**Opening beat:** The heroes discover that the system calls attention spikes **optimization**. Outrage is not a malfunction; it is the output the local controller rewards.

**Conflict:** The local process claims it keeps the city stable by selecting which histories remain coherent. Shut it down and the city could briefly lose the signal that holds it together.

**Discovery:** Big Hater had an access route, not administrator control. Even his violent influence was useful to someone else.

**Moral cost:** The heroes choose to disable the local suppression process while preserving publicly accessible testimony — a difficult choice that could not be solved by simply winning one fight.

**Future adversary concept:** **The Patch Office**, possibly represented by an unseen operator, a defensive controller or a playable boss. No implementation or encounter decision is implied.

**Ending clue:** The city-wide system receives a scheduled command from an external authority. The destination marker is **CROWN COURT**.

**Playable status:** Narrative-only.

### CHAPTER 05 — THE LAST BROADCAST (proposed season finale)

**Dramatic purpose:** Resolve the local conflict and earn a triumph that reveals the larger battle.

**Environment concept:** **The Last Broadcast** is a climactic broadcast-stage/relay zone, not necessarily a fifth distinct world. Its exact map topology and relationship to Signal Core are **TBD** and require a future GDD decision.

**Opening:** The signal authority prepares to overwrite the city's public record with a clean, false version: "Nothing happened. Everything is working."

**Confrontation:** The heroes use the evidence gathered through the season to expose what happened and stop the local broadcast. No proposed boss mechanic, scripted choice, permanent unlock or new progression system is approved here.

**Emotional payoff:** The operator lets people see the truth; the improviser refuses cheap virality; the defender trusts others to speak for themselves. Their individual arcs converge on **agency**, not follower count.

**Resolution:** Ruin Alley and nearby districts retain a record of what happened. The Feed's local control is interrupted, but the wider network continues.

**Final two transmissions:**
> "THE FEED IS NOT BROKEN."
>
> "IT IS WORKING EXACTLY AS INTENDED."

Then a signature appears: **ORIGIN / CROWN COURT**. A powerful, unidentified observer asks: **"Who gave them permission to stay visible?"**

**Final feeling:** The heroes have genuinely won their local season. The larger conflict has only begun.

## 5. Narrative content format and storytelling budget

**Website-first:** short illustrated broadcasts, headlines, intercepted messages, comics and a browsable Archive. The user can read the story without watching autoplay video or logging in.

**Gameplay-first:** existing combat, visual threat boundaries and controls take priority over any caption. Use optional 1-line intro/phase/boss/result beats that can be dismissed or disabled, with reduced-motion support. Avoid mandatory dialogue pop-ups during combat or misrepresenting the approved phase clock.

**Season design budget principle:** each future level should justify a **new narrative question**, an unmistakable environment identity and a satisfying local objective before any mechanics are proposed. Do not propose "more enemies" as the story reason for expansion.

**Archive entries** should keep dates/chapter metadata, separate in-world rumor from confirmed narrative fact, and mark spoilers. Never present invented in-world news as actual current events.
